    Model

    What the sim computes and what it leaves out

    Detection and cueing

    • Flight. Constant-Cd quadratic drag in closed form: v(s) = v0·e^(−s/L), L = 2m / (ρ·Cd·A). Rockets and drones fly at constant speed. Gravity drop and yaw are ignored over the last few hundred metres.
    • Camera signal. The round is a point target. Signal-to-noise per frame is contrast × pixel fill × peak-pixel energy ÷ σ, with pixel fill (π/4)(d / (R·IFOV))² capped at 1, divided by 1 + smear for angular motion during the exposure, and for thermal cores by the bolometer lag 1 + τ·2v/R.
    • Noise. Visible sensors are photon-limited: σ = noise × (f/2) ÷ √(light × exposure / 0.1 ms). Frame differencing leaves clutter in proportion to how far the background moves between frames after ego-motion compensation. Thermal noise is light-independent.
    • Detection and false alarms. Per-frame detection probability is Φ(SNR − k). A track confirms after M detections in N frames. Noise false alarms per hour are pixels × rate × Q(k) × C(N−1, M−1) × (9·Q(k))^(M−1). Clutter movers such as birds, insects and rain are not modelled.
    • Event cameras sample at their pixel latency (longer in low light), and their threshold maps to k = 5. Ego-motion drives the event rate and the compute load.
    • Bandwidth and compute throttle the frame rate when either is exceeded. MIPI pods beyond the host's CSI ports are dropped. Latency is half the exposure, plus camera output, transfer, processing, fusion and controller.
    • Radar. Range scales as R_ref × σ^¼. A waveform not built for ballistic targets loses coherent gain when the round crosses a range bin faster than a chirp loop: N_eff = ΔR ÷ (v × T_loop). Per-look Pd follows the R⁻⁴ law with 3 dB fluctuation. Chronographs report afterwards and give no bearing.

    Response, burden and readiness

    • Response chain. Alert, then driver latency, then slew to the cued bearing (dead time + angle ÷ speed, with an acceleration limit for steppers, slowed by gear reduction, limited by frame travel), then deployment.
    • Pneumatic deployment. Valve and line delays, then t = √(2·standoff ÷ a) with a = P·A_bore ÷ (element + carriage). Actuator deployment uses stroke ÷ speed, or a 90° swing for rotary parts, and checks force against the element's weight.
    • Geometry. Aim points spread over torso and head. The element is a flat plate at the standoff, pointed with cue bearing error plus actuator, frame and backlash error. Elevation shifts the crossing point. It protects only if the path crosses it and its level meets the threat's.
    • Mass and balance. Each item is placed on the body for head-borne mass and centre-of-mass shift against a 105 kg wearer with kit. Battery mass follows the chosen cell chemistry.
    • Power. Running power of sensors, compute and controller, plus idle or holding power of actuators and compressor duty, then 10% conversion loss and 90% usable capacity.
    • Readiness. The weakest rated part sets system readiness, as with TRL. Unrated parts are listed, not scored.
    • Validation hook. ANCILE reported 28.6 ± 8.3% interception of foam darts at 5–20 m/s, reliable at ≤ 7.5 m/s. Load the ANCILE example, set bearings to Bench and include the foam-dart threat to compare.

    Planning tool only. None of these parts has been shown to detect small-arms projectiles reliably in a wearable package. Results are the upper bound a simple optical and radar model allows; use them to steer bench tests, not to replace them. Library edits and saved builds live only in this browser.
