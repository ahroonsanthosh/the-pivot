## TI IWR6843AOP as a threat cue
<a id="ti-radar"></a>

**Verdict: Not supported.** Small arms not supported: at best 0% of rounds get 5 ms of warning. To cue a 5.56 mm round in half of shots the radar needs +90 dB more link budget as configured, or +72 dB with a ballistic waveform. Slower, larger targets: FPV 52%, 40 mm 0%.

Desk findings:

- TI IWR6843AOP: Verified: 60–64 GHz, 3 TX / 4 RX, 120° field of view, USB point-cloud output, US$172.10 from TI. No documented bullet-detection capability; ballistic suitability unknown.

*Question.* With the radar alone, facing the threat, how often does a cue arrive with at least the required warning? How much more sensitivity would small arms need?

*Method.* Radar-only build, one unit on the chest, threats from the front ±60°. Monte Carlo per threat, then a search for the sensitivity that would give a 5.56 mm round a timely cue in half of all rounds, with the point-cloud waveform and with a waveform that keeps coherent gain on fast targets.

| Threat | Detection range at speed | Same target, slow | Any cue | Cue ≥ 5 ms | Median warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9×19 mm pistol | 0.35 m | 0.97 m | 0% | 0% | — |
| 5.56×45 mm rifle | 0.30 m | 0.81 m | 0% | 0% | — |
| 7.62×51 mm rifle | 0.33 m | 0.91 m | 0% | 0% | — |
| 12.7×99 mm HMG | 0.47 m | 1.3 m | 0% | 0% | — |
| 40 mm low-velocity grenade | 1.1 m | 2.3 m | 0% | 0% | — |
| FPV attack drone | 2.5 m | 4.0 m | 66% | 52% | 14 ms |
| RPG-type rocket (reference) | 1.7 m | 4.6 m | 0% | 0% | — |

What the sim shows:

- The point-cloud waveform smears a 940 m/s round across range bins: detection range on 5.56 mm falls from 0.81 m for a slow target of the same size to 0.30 m.
- One unit watches 33% of the ground band; 3 units are needed round the body.

What would change the verdict:

- Range goes as the fourth root of RCS × sensitivity: a ×10 error in the estimated bullet RCS moves range by ×1.8 (10 dB). Neither the 10 m reference range nor the bullet RCS has been measured; a range-gated bench recording of a fired round would settle both.

Both cue grenades and drones. The xiQ is end-of-life, so the procurable Daheng needs more USB bandwidth or a smaller ROI to match it.

Desk findings:

- Pixel angle sets the limit: a 5.56 mm round fills a whole pixel only within a few metres, where the round has a few milliseconds left to fly.

- Teensy 4.1: Verified: 600 MHz Cortex-M7, 55 digital I/O, 3 CAN interfaces, USB host and Ethernet.
- NXP FRDM-A-S32K344 (S32K3): Verified: active NXP development board, US$60, in stock when checked. The S32K3 family supports up to ASIL D, which does not make a finished system ASIL D compliant.

*Method.* Response-time budget for a 60° slew plus pneumatic deployment of the ANCILE sail, with each controller on two drive chains: direct step/dir (TMC2209) and ANCILE's CNC shield under GRBL.

What the sim shows:

- The FRDM-A-S32K344 is a development board; a product would put the MCU on its own PCB.

## Kevlar sail 100 × 100 mm: how much of the body it covers
<a id="sail-coverage"></a>

Desk findings:

- Kevlar sail 100 × 100 mm: Verified: 100 × 100 mm, about 40 g. Material tested separately; complete-system ballistic protection not demonstrated.

*Question.* With perfect, instant pointing, what share of rounds aimed at the torso and head cross the sail, and what size would cover 90%?

*Method.* A fixed element 250 mm in front of the wearer, rounds from straight ahead aimed anywhere on the torso and head. Sizes are swept; mass follows the sail's areal density, and pneumatic deployment uses the ANCILE source, valve and line.

| Element | Mass at 4 kg/m² | Path crosses it | Pneumatic deploy, 250 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 × 100 mm | 40 g | 5% | 38 ms |
| 200 × 200 mm | 160 g | 15% | 48 ms |
| 300 × 400 mm | 480 g | 49% | 65 ms |
| 400 × 600 mm | 960 g | 84% | 82 ms |

What the sim shows:

- The sail stopped .22 LR to .357 Magnum on a pendulum. That is a material result; whole-system protection was not demonstrated.

What would change the verdict:

- The aim-point spread is the main assumption. With fire concentrated on the chest (aim spread ×0.5) the 100 × 100 mm share is 16% instead of 5%.
