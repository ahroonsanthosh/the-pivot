Concept of use

How a wearer would run it

  1. Kit up. Pods clip to helmet rails, shoulder straps or the plate carrier. Compute and battery ride in a rear pouch; one USB3 harness per pod runs down the carrier.
  2. Power on and self-test. The Teensy checks each camera heartbeat, radar link and battery state, then shows a green status LED.
  3. Background model. The Jetson learns a few seconds of static scene per pod so frame differencing can pick out fast movers.
  4. Watch. Every pod streams at full rate. A candidate is anything resolvable that grows or moves between frames; the radar adds an independent close-range cue in front.
  5. Confirm and cue. After the confirm count is met, the Jetson sends a bearing to the Teensy, which drives a haptic buzz and LED in the direction of the threat and timestamps the event.
  6. Log and review. Raw frames around every event are kept for after-action review and for building the training set the detector currently lacks.
Model assumptions

What this sim simplifies

  • Camera detection needs the round to fill a set fraction of one pixel (diameter × contrast ÷ pixel angle ≥ threshold), then be seen in N consecutive frames.
  • Rounds fly straight at constant speed toward the torso. Drag, yaw and muzzle-flash cues are ignored.
  • Bandwidth and compute limits throttle the frame rate when exceeded: usable USB3 is 8 Gbps on the Orin dev kit and 4 Gbps on the Pi 5; classical detection throughput is 1,100 / 1,800 / 150 Mpixel/s for Orin 15 W, Orin 25 W and Pi 5.
  • Latency after the confirming frame = 0.1 ms exposure + USB transfer + per-frame processing + 0.05 ms Teensy output.
  • Radar range scales with radar cross-section to the ¼ power from 15 m on a 1 m² target, at 30 frames/s, ±60° in front, with 10 ms processing. Bullet speeds are far past its unambiguous Doppler limit (≈21 m/s), so it can at best flag presence.
  • Mass includes lens, housing and harness per pod, a compute enclosure, and Li-ion at 150 Wh/kg.
  • RPG-type rocket is included as a reference point: it is the class of threat vehicle active protection systems are actually built against.

Planning tool only. None of these parts has been shown to detect small-arms projectiles reliably; results here are the upper bound a simple optical and radar model allows, and are meant to steer bench tests, not replace them.
